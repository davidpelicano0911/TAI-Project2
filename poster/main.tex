\headerbox{\color{white}1.\ Motivation \& Problem}{name=motiv,column=0,row=0}{

  Modern telescopes produce \textbf{16-bit raw image streams} where storage
  and transmission bandwidth are critical.  General-purpose compressors
  (gzip, xz, zstd) treat pixels as bytes, ignoring spatial structure.

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Our goal:} design lossless codecs that exploit the unique statistics
  of astronomical imagery:

  \smallskip
  \begin{itemize}\compactlist
    \item Smooth, near-uniform backgrounds (high spatial correlation)
    \item Sparse bright point sources (sparse residuals)
    \item 16-bit dynamic range --- byte-split creates correlated pairs
    \item Fixed $1500\!\times\!1500$-pixel frames (repeatable block geometry)
  \end{itemize}

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Design axes:} compression ratio $\leftrightarrow$ speed
  $\leftrightarrow$ simplicity.  We present \textbf{three codecs} on this
  Pareto frontier.

  \tightsection
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.82,font=\small]

\headerbox{\color{white}2.\ Common Architecture}{name=arch,column=0,below=motiv,above=bottom}{

  All three codecs share a \textbf{predict-residualise-encode} pipeline:

  \smallskip
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=0.45cm,
      box/.style={draw,rounded corners=3pt,fill=blue!8,
                  minimum width=1.58cm,minimum height=0.62cm,
                  align=center,font=\scriptsize}]
    \node[box] (px)  {Raw pixel\\$p_{x,y}$};
    \node[box,right=0.35cm of px] (pr) {Predictor\\$\hat{p}$};
    \node[box,right=0.35cm of pr] (re) {Residual\\$r=p-\hat{p}$};
    \node[box,right=0.35cm of re] (en) {Entropy\\coder};
    \draw[->] (px) -- (pr);
    \draw[->] (pr) -- (re);
    \draw[->] (re) -- (en);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \end{center}

  \smallskip
  Residuals are \textbf{zigzag-mapped} to non-negative integers and split into
  a high byte (hi) and low byte (lo) before entropy coding.
  The codecs differ in \emph{predictor} and \emph{entropy coder}.
}

%% =====================================================================
%% BOX: PRISM-Block (fast)
%% =====================================================================
\headerbox{\color{white}3.\ PRISM-Block — Fast Codec}{name=prism,column=1,row=0}{

  \textbf{Predictor — GAP (Gradient-Adaptive):}
  {\small
  \[
    \hat{p}=\frac{(\delta_v+1)W+(\delta_h+1)N}{\delta_v+\delta_h+2},
    \quad
    \begin{gathered}
      \delta_h=|W-NW|\\[-0.2em]
      \delta_v=|N-NW|
    \end{gathered}
  \]
  }
  Uses only causal neighbours ($W$, $N$, $NW$), with edge cases biased toward
  the stronger horizontal or vertical gradient.

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Entropy coder — Adaptive Range Coder:}
  \begin{itemize}\compactlist
    \item Fenwick-tree frequency model (256 symbols, rescales at $2^{16}$)
    \item \textbf{9 contexts:} 1 for hi-byte; 8 for lo-byte conditioned on
          local residual magnitude $m_W + m_N$
    \item LZMA-style carry-propagation encoder
  \end{itemize}

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Parallelism — Independent Row Blocks:}
  \begin{itemize}\compactlist
    \item Image split into 150-row horizontal bands
    \item Each band encoded independently $\Rightarrow$ zero inter-thread sync
    \item \texttt{std::thread} pool, as many threads as CPU cores
  \end{itemize}

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Micro-optimisations:}
  \begin{itemize}\compactlist
    \item First-row and first-pixel cases peeled from inner loop
    \item Context lookup via precomputed \texttt{LO\_CTX\_TAB[1024]}
    \item Works on \emph{any} image dimensions via \texttt{infer\_dims()}
  \end{itemize}

  \tightsection
  \begin{center}\small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
    \toprule
    & \textbf{Compress} & \textbf{Decompress}\\
    \midrule
    Avg.\ time & \textbf{35\,ms} & \textbf{46\,ms}\\
    Avg.\ ratio & 3.51\,b/B & ---\\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
}

%% =====================================================================
%% BOX: HAIS2 (all-rounder)
%% =====================================================================
\headerbox{\color{white}4.\ HAIS2 — All-Rounder Codec}{name=hais,column=2,row=0}{

  \textbf{Block-Adaptive Multi-Predictor:}\\
  Each $\mathrm{BS}\!\times\!\mathrm{BS}$ block independently selects
  the predictor with minimum estimated entropy:

  \smallskip
  \begin{center}\scriptsize
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}clll@{}}
    \toprule
    Mode & Name & Predictor & Overhead\\
    \midrule
    0 & raw   & identity  & 0 B\\
    1 & avg   & $(W+N+NW)/3$ & 0 B\\
    3 & mean  & global mean $\mu$ & 0 B\\
    4 & LS4   & LS$(W,N,NW,1)$ & 16 B\\
    6 & LS5   & LS$(W,N,NW,NE,NN)$ & 20 B\\
    9 & LS6   & LS$(W,N,NW,WW,NN,1)$ & 24 B\\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \smallskip
  LS weights are solved per block.  The estimator scores
  $H(\mathrm{hi})+\frac{n_0}{N}H(\mathrm{lo}|h=0)+
  \frac{n_1}{N}H(\mathrm{lo}|h>0)$.

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Entropy Coder — ANS/FSE:}
  \begin{itemize}\compactlist
    \item Static per-block frequency table (sparse: $5\!\times\!\mathrm{nnz}$ bytes,
          dense: 1025 bytes)
    \item Hi and lo streams encoded separately
    \item Optional \emph{ctx} mode: lo split into $h\!=\!0$ / $h\!>\!0$ sub-streams
  \end{itemize}

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Block size selection:} largest $\mathrm{BS}\!\leq\!512$ dividing both
  $W$ and $H$ with $\geq\!4$ blocks.  For $1500\!\times\!1500$: $\mathrm{BS}=500$
  (9 blocks).  Smaller blocks lose to FSE table overhead.

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Predictor distribution on test set:}
  \begin{center}\scriptsize
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcl@{}}
    \toprule
    File type & Winner & Notes\\
    \midrule
    Star field (A) & avg (9/9) & 4.39\,b/B\\
    Smooth bg (C,D,G,H) & mean/LS6 & 2.41--2.73\,b/B\\
    High contrast (F) & LS6 (9/9) & 6.11\,b/B\\
    Sparse stars (E) & raw+mean & 2.57\,b/B\\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
}

%% BOX: HAIS2+ (in development)
%% =====================================================================
\headerbox{\color{white}5.\ HAIS2+ — Best Ratio (In Development)}{name=future,column=3,row=0}{

  \textbf{Known residual gaps (from information theory analysis):}
  \begin{itemize}\compactlist
    \item File A: lag-1 autocorrelation of residuals after avg = \textbf{0.79}
          (raw = 0.95), so spatial structure remains.
    \item Hi/lo byte split overhead $\approx$ 22\,KB total across 8 files
    \item File A: 8.77\,b/B achieved vs 8.70\,b/B theoretical
  \end{itemize}

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Planned improvements:}
  \begin{enumerate}\compactlist
    \item \textbf{CALIC-style bias correction} --- per-context mean correction
          before entropy coding.
    \item \textbf{16-bit FSE table} for hi==0 blocks --- eliminates the
          byte-split overhead for smooth regions.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive LS predictor} --- online weight update using
          running covariance.
  \end{enumerate}

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Why not GAP or MED?} Analysis confirmed these never win on
  this data: astronomical images are either smooth (mean/LS wins) or star-field
  (avg wins).  Including them adds latency with zero gain.

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Current status:} bias correction prototype tested; results pending.
  Expected gain: $\Delta \approx 0.02$--$0.05$\,b/B on star-field images.
}

\headerbox{\color{white}7.\ Conclusions}{name=concl,column=3,below=future,above=bottom}{

  \textbf{Key takeaways:}
  \begin{itemize}\compactlist
    \item Domain knowledge (spatial prediction, byte-split entropy) beats
          general-purpose compressors by $0.2$--$1.0$\,b/B while running faster.
    \item Predictor selection per block is the single highest-impact design
          choice; LS6 dominates on smooth and gradient regions.
    \item Residual autocorrelation (0.79 for star fields) remains the main
          open challenge, targeted by HAIS2+.
  \end{itemize}

  \tightsection
  \textbf{Recommendations by use case:}
  \begin{center}\scriptsize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Scenario} & \textbf{Codec}\\
    \midrule
    Real-time acquisition & PRISM-Block\\
    Archival storage & HAIS2\\
    Maximum ratio & HAIS2+ (dev) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \tightsection
  \textbf{All code is original} (C++17, no third-party compression libraries).
  Benchmark comparisons invoke external tools as separate processes.
}

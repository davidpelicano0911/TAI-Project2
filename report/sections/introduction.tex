\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Raw images produced by ground-based astronomical telescopes are large,
structured numerical arrays. Each image in this study is a $1500\times1500$
raster of unsigned 16-bit pixels stored in big-endian order, occupying
4.5\,MB. These arrays are not photographs in the conventional sense: they
contain a spatially smooth sky background, a constant bias pedestal added by
the readout electronics, stars or other point sources, and shot noise that
grows with signal intensity. This structure differs markedly from text,
executables, or multimedia files, so general-purpose compressors such as
\texttt{gzip} or \texttt{zstd} cannot exploit it.

The spatial smoothness means that a pixel value is well predicted by its
causal neighbours. The bias pedestal means that subtracting a global mean
removes most of the DC component before entropy coding. These two
observations motivate \emph{predictive} compression: encode the
residual after prediction rather than the pixel itself, so that the residual
distribution is narrower and the entropy is lower.

We present two lossless compressors that exploit these properties, targeting
distinct points on the speed--ratio trade-off:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{prism-block} applies a gradient-adjusted predictor (GAP) to
    the full 16-bit value, codes the residual with a range coder driven by
    eight context-conditioned adaptive models, and parallelises across
    horizontal bands. It prioritises throughput.
  \item \textbf{HAIS} divides the image into square blocks, selects the best
    of five candidate predictors per block (including training-free
    least-squares), splits residuals into independent byte streams, and codes
    them with Finite State Entropy (FSE/tANS)~\cite{collet2013fse}. It
    prioritises compression ratio.
\end{itemize}

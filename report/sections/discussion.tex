\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Generic compressors treat the two bytes of each 16-bit pixel as unrelated,
breaking the spatial correlation that spans pixel boundaries in big-endian
encoding. Our codecs operate on 16-bit values directly, predict each pixel
from its decoded neighbours, and entropy-code only the residual, yielding a
reduction of $0.87$--$1.01$\,bpp over gzip-6 depending on the codec.

The predictor turns out to be the dominant design choice. Table~\ref{tab:modes}
shows the per-block mode selection produced by \allrounder{} on each benchmark
image. Mode~3 (gmean) dominates on four of eight images: it is the sole
predictor for images D and H, which are bias frames with a near-constant DC
pedestal, and the majority predictor for E and G. Images A and B, which have
smooth or mixed stellar backgrounds, are handled by Mode~1 (avg), which better
tracks slowly varying spatial gradients. Image~F is the exception --- a science
frame containing bright point sources and extended emission where LS-4 and LS-5
account for all nine blocks, yielding the largest per-image gain over generics
($0.33$\,bpp over bzip2-9 for \allrounder{}). Image~C uses Mode~0 (raw) on six
blocks, suggesting that dark calibration frames with dominant Poisson noise lose
spatial correlation at block scale. Quantitatively, prediction accounts for
$0.67$\,bpp of the $0.78$\,bpp gain over zstd-9; the remaining $0.11$\,bpp
comes from the choice of entropy coder, confirming that the predictor is the
binding component.

On the entropy coding side, splitting residuals into independent hi8 and lo8
streams is the key structural choice. After a good prediction, hi8 is
near-uniformly zero and is often compressed to a 2-byte FSE packet, leaving
all meaningful variation to the lo8 stream. \spdfocused{} achieves the same
effect through eight adaptive context models without transmitting a frequency
table, trading $0.11$\,bpp for a roughly $2\times$ speed advantage over
\allrounder{} (49 vs 96\,ms). \cmpfocused{}'s exhaustive $10\times6$ search
recovers only $0.03$\,bpp over \allrounder{}, which further confirms that
predictor quality is the binding constraint rather than the entropy coder.

To assess generalisation, we tested \allrounder{} on the dataset from
Quintas-Torra et al.~\cite{quintas2026} (INT, JKT, LCO, TJO, and WHT
telescopes; 225 images with dimensions ranging from $510\times765$ to
$4200\times2154$). Mean bpp per telescope was INT~2.71, JKT~2.85, TJO~2.78,
WHT~3.07, and LCO~3.36. LCO is the hardest dataset, mixing full-frame science
images with small calibration frames whose shorter spatial runs give the
predictor less context, pushing bpp up to 6.88 in the worst case. WHT also
compresses less efficiently due to high raw entropy driven by dense stellar
fields and strong read-noise, while INT and TJO benefit from large uniform sky
backgrounds and stable bias pedestals. These results confirm that the codecs
generalise across sensor geometries and telescope types without any retraining.

Overall, all three codecs outperform every tested generic compressor in
compression ratio, and two of the three also beat bzip2-9 in total runtime.
The consistent margin across all eight benchmark images, together with the
generalisation to 225 external images from five telescopes, confirms that the
gains are structural rather than dataset-specific. Both encoder and decoder
accept explicit row and column dimensions, making the codecs applicable to any
sensor geometry.

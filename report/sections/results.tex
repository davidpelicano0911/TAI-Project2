\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Compression Ratio}

Table~\ref{tab:bpp} reports bits per byte for each image.
Both domain-specific codecs outperform every generic compressor on every
image. HAIS achieves the best ratio overall, beating zstd-19 by
$0.60$\,bpp on average ($-15\%$) and bzip2-9 by $0.18$\,bpp.
prism-block is slightly less efficient ($+0.11$\,bpp over HAIS) but still
surpasses all generics by at least $0.07$\,bpp.
Image F (high-dynamic-range stellar field) is the hardest for every codec;
image G (smooth blank-sky frame) is the easiest.

\subsection{Speed}

Table~\ref{tab:speed} shows average per-image compress and decompress times.
prism-block is the fastest compressor in the whole suite: 81\,ms total,
comparable to zstd-3 (84\,ms) but with $0.85$\,bpp better compression.
HAIS takes 187\,ms total — roughly $2.3\times$ slower than prism-block but
$3.4\times$ faster than bzip2-9 and $13\times$ faster than xz-9,
while achieving better or equal compression in every case.

\subsection{Pareto Analysis}
\label{sec:pareto}

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} plots all benchmarked compressors on the
bpp-vs-time plane (log scale). The Pareto frontier — the set of compressors
for which no other codec is simultaneously faster \emph{and} more
compressive — passes through prism-block and HAIS. Both occupy distinct and
non-dominated positions: prism-block dominates all generic tools in both
dimensions simultaneously; HAIS achieves the best overall compression ratio
while remaining faster than every generic codec except zstd-1/3.
